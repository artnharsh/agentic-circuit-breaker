# Climate Change: Overview and Causes

## What Is Climate Change?

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns.
While some climate change is natural, since the mid-20th century human activities —
primarily the burning of fossil fuels — have been the dominant driver of observed warming.

## The Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is a natural process where gases in Earth's atmosphere trap heat
from the Sun. The primary greenhouse gases (GHGs) are:
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂): released by burning coal, oil, and natural gas; deforestation
- Methane (CH₄): released from livestock, landfills, and natural gas leaks
- Nitrous oxide (N₂O): released from agricultural fertilizers and combustion
- Water vapor: the most abundant GHG, amplifies the warming from other gases
- Fluorinated gases: synthetic gases used in refrigeration and manufacturing

## Global Temperature Trends

According to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC):
- Global average surface temperature has increased by approximately 1.1°C above
  pre-industrial levels (1850–1900 baseline) as of 2023.
- The last decade (2011–2020) was the warmest on record.
- Current policies put the world on track for approximately 2.5–3°C of warming
  by 2100.

## Carbon Dioxide Concentrations

Atmospheric CO₂ concentration reached approximately 421 parts per million (ppm) in 2023,
the highest level in at least 3 million years. Pre-industrial levels were around 280 ppm.

## Renewable Energy as a Mitigation Strategy

Transitioning from fossil fuels to renewable energy is considered one of the most critical
levers for limiting climate change. Renewable energy sources (solar, wind, hydro)
produce negligible direct CO₂ emissions during operation.

The IPCC estimates that reaching net-zero CO₂ emissions by approximately 2050 is
necessary to limit warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.
