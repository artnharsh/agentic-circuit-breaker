# Renewable Energy: Wind and Hydropower

## Wind Energy

Wind turbines convert kinetic energy from moving air into electricity. Modern onshore
wind turbines typically have rotor diameters of 100–150 meters and hub heights of
80–120 meters. Capacity factors for wind vary widely:
- Onshore wind: 25–45% depending on location
- Offshore wind: 35–55%, benefiting from stronger and more consistent winds

Global installed wind capacity reached approximately 2.1 TW at the end of 2023.
Wind energy generated about 2,300 TWh globally in 2022.

## Hydropower

Hydropower is the largest source of renewable electricity globally, generating
approximately 4,300 TWh in 2022 — about 15% of total global electricity generation.

Conventional hydropower uses dams to store water in reservoirs. Run-of-river
hydropower generates electricity from the natural flow of rivers without large
reservoirs.

## Energy Storage

The intermittent nature of solar and wind power requires energy storage solutions:
- **Lithium-ion batteries**: The dominant technology for short-duration storage (hours)
- **Pumped hydro storage**: Accounts for ~90% of global grid-scale storage capacity
- **Hydrogen**: Emerging option for long-duration storage

## Combined Renewable Capacity

As of 2023, renewables (solar, wind, hydro, biomass, geothermal) account for
approximately 33% of global electricity generation, up from 26% in 2015.
The International Energy Agency (IEA) projects renewables to supply 42% of
global electricity by 2030 under current policies.
